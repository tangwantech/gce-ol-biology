Viruses

1. List the general characteristics of viruses.

  • Viruses are microscopic and can only be seen with the help of an electron microscope.
  • They have either DNA or RNA as their genetic material but never both.
  • They lack cell surface membrane, cyctoplasm, nucleus and other cell organelles.
  • They are inert outside a living cell and only become active while inside the host.
  • They are obligate intracellular parasites.
  • They are unable to carry out metabolic processes such as respiration, excretion. However, they can reproduce but only inside a living host cell.

2. Why are viruses sometimes considered as non-living?

Viruses are sometimes considered as non living for the following reasons:

  • They are inert outside a living cell.
  • They can be crystallised like non-living chemicals.
  • They are unable to carryout metabolic processes such as respiration, excretion, nutrition, which are characteristic of living cells.
  • They lack cell surface membrane, cytoplasm and other organelles characteristic of living cells.

3. Draw a large and well labelled diagram of a T2 bacteriophage. State the functions of the parts labelled.

T2 bacteriophage

Functions of parts:

  • Protein coat: It protects the genetic material (DNA).
  • Genetic material: It carries instructions to make new viral particles.
  • Core: It acts as a channel for viral DNA to enter a host cell.
  • Tail sheath: It penetrates the host cell surface membrane inorder for the viral genetic material to enter the host cell.
  • Tail plate and tail fibres: They enables the viral particle to adhere to the surface of the host cell.

4. Describe the lifecycle of a T2 bacteriophage.

  1. The virus approaches a bacterium.
  2. The virus attaches itself to the bacterium using its tail fibres.
  3. The tail sheath is pushed through the cell wall and cell surface membrane of the bacterium.
  4. The viral DNA enters the bacterium leaving the empty protein coat and tail outside the bacterial cell wall.
  5. The viral DNA takes over the protein synthesizing machinery of the host cell.
  6. New viral DNA molecules are synthesized within the bacterium.
  7. Viral DNA molecules become surrounded by protein coat synthesized by the host cell under the instruction of the viral DNA.
  8. Bacterium ruptures and its contents together with the new mature viral particles are released into the environment.
  9. Each viral particle may go on to infect new bacteria and the cycle is repeated.

5. State the economic importance of viruses.

  1. Useful effects
    • Viruses can be used in research to produce vaccines e.g. Polio vaccine.
    • Bacteriophages attack and destroy disease causing bacteria.
    • Viruses can be used in genetic engineering as vectors (carriers) of important genes to be cloned.
    • Some viruses may be used for biological control e.g. Myxomatosis virus for controlling rabbit population in the wild.
  2. Harmful effects
    • Some viruses cause diseases to man e.g. AIDS, Poliomyelitis, Ebola
    • Some viruses destroy plants which are of economic value e.g. tobacco mosaic virus which destroys tobacco plants.

6. Write short notes about any three viral diseases under the following headings: Name of disease, Causative agent, Method of transmission, Signs and symptoms and Methods of prevention

I. HIV/AIDS (Acquired immune deficiency syndrome)

Causative agent: HIV (Human immuno virus)

Method of spread (transmission):

  • Through unprotected sexual intercourse with an infected person.
  • Through infected blood transfusion.
  • Through sharing of infected blades, syringes, needles or surgical materials
  • From infected mother to child during pregnancy or after birth through breast milk.

Signs and symptoms: Weight loss, persistent fever, prolonged dry cough, reduced immunity.

Method of prevention:

  • Abstinence
  • Use of condoms during sexual intercourse with suspected person.
  • Blood should be properly screened before transfusion.
  • Do not share blades, syringes and needles.
  • Sterilise surgical instrument before use.
  • Avoid sexual promiscuity.
  • Infected pregnant women should take drugs which can prevent the transmission of the virus to the baby

II. Poliomyelitis

Causative agent: Polio virus

Method of spread (transmission):

  • Through contaminated water and food

Signs and symptoms: Fever, headache, stifness of neck and other muscles.

Method of prevention:

  • VAccination
  • Boiling water before use.
  • Good food and personal hygiene

III. Influenza

Causative agent: Haemophilus Influenzae

Method of spread (transmission):

  • Airbone.

Signs and symptoms: Fever, headache, loss of appetite.

Method of prevention:

  • Ensure good ventilation.
  • Avoid overcrowding.
  • Cover your nose and mouth while sneezing
