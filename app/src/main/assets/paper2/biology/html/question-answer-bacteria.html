Bacteria

1. List the general characterisitcs of bacteria

  • They are microspic and unicellular.
  • They reproduce primarily by binary fission.
  • They have a single circular DNA not surrounded by a nuclear membrane.
  • They have fewer organelles and none is surrounded by a double membrane.
  • Cell wall is present with murein as the main strengthening compound.
  • Special infoldings of the cell surface membrane called mesosomes are used for respiration
  • Protein synthesis is carried out on 70S ribosomes
  • Additional pieces of circular DNA called plasmids may be present in certain bacteria. Plasmids are separate from the main DNA.
  • Some bacteria may have one or more flagella. However, they are simple and lacking microtubules.

Functions of parts:

  • DNA: It controls all the activities of the cell.
  • Plasmid: It confers additional survival advantages such as resistance to antibiotics, ability to use complex chemicals as food.
  • 70S ribosomes: They are sites of protein synthesis.
  • Slime capsule: It provides additional protection, it allows bacteria to stick to surfaces.
  • flagella: It is used for locomotion
  • Cell surface membrane: It regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell; it may be folded to form mesosomes for respiration.
  • Cell wall: It prevents the cell from bursting when it absorbs water by osmosis; it allows passage of water, ions and other smaller molecules into and out of the cell.
  • Cytoplasm It is the site of many metabolic reactions.

3. Write short notes on the mode of life of bacteria under the following heading: Reproduction, respiration, population growth..

  1. Reproduction:

    2. Reproduction in bacteria is primarily asexual by binary fission.

    When a bacterium reaches a certain size, its DNA replicates to produce another identical copy.

    The cytoplasm splits into two, giving rise to two new identical daughter cells.

  2. Respiration:

    3. Respiration in bacteria can be aerobic or anaerobic.

    Aerobic respiration involves the complete break down of glucose using oxygen to prodce much energy (ATP), water and carbon dioxide. Aerobic respiration occurs in the cytoplasm and on mesosomes (infoldings of the cell surface membrane). Bacteria lack mitochondria.

    Anaerobic respiration involves the partial break down of glucose without the use of oxygen. It occurs in the cytoplasm and produces far less energy, carbon dioxide and alcohol or lactic acid.

    Bacteria which require aerobic condition only are called obligate aerobes. Bacteria which require anaerobic condition only are called obligate anaerobes. Bacteria that can survive in both aerobic and anaerobic conditions are called facultative aerobes

  3. Bacterial population growth:

    4. Bacterial population growth shows four distinct phases

    1. Lag phase:

      2. Bacteria are adapting to their new environment e.g. by synthesizing new enzymes. Growth has not yet achieved its maximum rate.

    2. Log phase:

      3. Bacteria are fully adapted to their new environment, nutrients are in abundant supply. Growth is progressing at its maximum rate.

    3. Stationary phase:

      4. Growth of the bacterial conlony begins to slow down and growth rate becomes zero. Dead rate becomes equal to rate of multiplication. This phase is as a result of several factors such as: exhaustion of nutrients, accumulation of toxic wastes, and if the bacteria are aerobes, shortage of oxygen.

    4. Phase of decline:

      5. Nutrients are exhausted, toxic wastes are in high concentrations. Eventually the cells stop multiplying all together. Death rate increases and becomes greater than the rate of multiplication.

5. List the economic importance of bacteria

  1. Useful effects:
    • Saprophytic bacteria help in the decay of dead plants and animals, recycling nutrients like carbon dioxide, water and ammonia into the environment.
    • Some bacteria are sources of antibiotics e.g. Streptomycin produced by Streptomyces.
    • E.coli in the colon of man produce vitamin K which is important in prothrombin (blood clotting factor) production in the liver.
    • Mutualistic bacteria in the stomach and caecum of herbivores produces cellulase which digest cellulose to glucose. No mammal is able to produce cellulase.
    • Nitrogen fixing bacteria in the soil e.g. Rhizobium and Nitrobacter increase soil fertility by converting atmospheric nitrogen into soil nitrates.
    • Some bacteria e.g. Lactobacillus are used in the production of yoghurt and cheese by fermenting milk sugar lactose to lactic acid which gives these dairy products their sour taste.
    • Azotobacter is used in the production of vinegar by converting ethanol to ethanoic acid.
    • E.coli is used in genetic engineering to produce human hormones e.g. insulin
    • Some bacteria are able to digest complex chemicals such as hydrocarbons making them useful in cleaning oil spilage from tankers
  2. Harmful effects:
    • Parasitic bacteria cause diseases to man e.g Typhoid, cholera, dysentery.
    • Some bacteria cause food spoilage e.g. Clostridium botulinum
    • Denitrifying bacteria e.g. Pseudomonas reduce soil fertility by converting soil nitrates into atmospheric nitrogen.
    • Some soil bacteria e.g. Streptomyces attack root vegetables such as carrots, potatoes.

6. Write short notes about any three bacterial diseases under the following headings: Name of disease, Causative agent, Method of transmission, Signs and symptoms, and Methods of prevention

  1. Cholera

    Causative agent: Vibrio cholerae

    Method of transmission: By drinking contaminated water or eating contaminated food (water and food borne)

    Signs and symptoms: Vomiting, watery diarrhoea, high fever

    Methods of prevention:

    • Supply safe and clean drinking water.
    • Avoid drinking contaminated water or eating contaminated food.
    • Boil water before drinking.
    • Cooked food should be properly covered to prevent contamination by vectors such as flies.
    • Ensure proper hygiene while preparing and cooking food.
    • Vaccination

  2. Typhoid

    Causative agent: Salmonella typhi

    Method of transmission: By drinking contaminated water or eating contaminated food (water and food borne)

    Signs and symptoms: High fever, diarrhoea, headache, abdominal pain

    Methods of prevention:

    • Supply safe and clean drinking water.
    • Avoid drinking contaminated water or eating contaminated food.
    • Boil water before drinking.
    • Cooked food should be properly covered to prevent contamination by vectors such as flies.
    • Ensure proper hygiene while preparing and cooking food.
    • Vaccination

  3. Gonorrhoea

    Causative agent: Neisseria gonorrhea

    Method of transmission:Through sexual intercourse with infected person

    Signs and symptoms:

    • In women, slight discharge and discomfort in urethra
    • In males, itching and burning sensation when passing out urine, yellow discharge of pus from the penis

    Methods of prevention:

    • Abstinence
    • Use of condom during sexual intercourse with suspected person.
    • Avoid sexual promiscuity.
    • Sexual partners should be simultaneously treated to avoid reinfection.

  4. Tuberculosis

    Causative agent: Mycobacterium tuberculosis

    Method of transmission: By breathing contaminated air (airbone)

    Signs and symptoms:: Cough (sometimes with sputum containing blood), lesions in the lung tissues, Weight loss.

    Methods of prevention:

    • Vaccination
    • Ensure good ventilation
    • Avoid overcrowding
