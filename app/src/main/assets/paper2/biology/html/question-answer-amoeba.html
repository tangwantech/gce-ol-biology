Amoeba

1. Draw a large and well labelled diagram of an amoeba. State the functions of the parts labelled.

Diagram of an amoeba

Functions of parts:

  • Nucleus: It contains genetic material (DNA) which controls all the activities of the cell.
  • Pseudopodium: Its is used for locomotion and ingestion of food particles
  • Cytoplasm It is the site of many metabolic reactions.
  • Food vacuoles: These are temporary and used to digested food.
  • Cell surface membrane: It regulates the movement of substances in and out of the cell.
  • Contractile vacuole: It helps in excretion and osmoregulation.

2. Describe the mode of life of an amoeba under the following headings: Movement, Nutrition, Gaseous exchange, Excretion, Osmoregulation, Reproduction

  1. Movement:
    • Amoeba moves using pseudopodia. These are not permanent structures but appear and disappear all the time.
    • The amoeba puts out its pseudopodia in the direction in which it wants to move and its entire body flows in that direction.
  2. Nutrition:
    • Nutrition in amoeba is heterotrophic. It feeds on smaller micro-organisms and decayed food particles present in water.
    • When it finds a food particle, it puts out its pseudopodia.
    • Pseudopodia flows around the food and engulfs it together with some water to form a food vacuole. This process is called ingestion.
    • Digestive enzymes are secreted into the food vacuole to digest the food particles.
    • The useful products of digestion and used by the amoeba for metabolism while any undigested food is egested as the amoeba moves away from the spot.
  3. Excretion:
    • Excretion of metabolic wastes such as carbon dioxide from respiration is carried out by simple diffusion from the cytoplasm across the entire cell surface membrane into the surrounding water.
    • In fresh water amoeba, excess water is excreted using contractile vacuoles.
  4. Osmoregulation
    • Osmoregulation in fresh water amoeba occurs with the help contractile vacuoles.
    • The cytoplasm is more concentrated than the surrounding water. Much water therefore enters the cyctoplasm by osmosis.
    • Excess water gets collected in contractile vacuoles. The size of the contractile vacuole increases as more water from the cytoplasm enters it.
    • The contractile vacuole flows towards the cell surface membrane, fuses with it and discharges the excess water into the surrounding.
    • Osmoregulation is an active process which requires energy in the form of ATP.
    • Osmoregulation does not take place in marine amoeba because its cytoplasm is isotonic to the surrounding salt water. There is no net movement of water into or out of the cell. Hence contractile vacuoles are absent in marine amoeba.
  5. Reproduction
    • Reproduction in amoeba is primarily asexual by binary fission.
    • When the amoeba reaches a certain size, its DNA replicates, followed by nuclear division.
    • The cytoplasm then divides tanversely giving rise to two new identical daughter cells.

3. Explain what would happen if a marine amoeba is placed in fresh water.

  • The cytoplasm of marine amoeba is more concentrated than fresh water. Much water therefore enters its cytoplasm through the cell surface membrane by osmosis.
  • The volume of the cell increases.
  • Since marine amoeba lacks contractile vacuoles to remove excess water, and also because of the absence of cell wall to resist expansion of the cell, the amoeba will eventually burst.
