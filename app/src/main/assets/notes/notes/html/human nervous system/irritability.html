Human nervous system

The nervous system allows organisms to perceive their environment, process information, and respond appropriately, ensuring survival and adaptation. It consists of the central nervous system (CNS) and the peripheral nervous system (PNS). The CNS includes the brain and spinal cord, while the PNS comprises nerves that connect the CNS to the rest of the body.

Irritability

Irritability is the ability of living organisms to respond to stimuli. It is a fundamental characteristic of life and is exhibited by all living things. The nervous system plays a crucial role in mediating these responses, allowing organisms to detect and react to changes in their environment.

Stimuli

Receptors

Receptors are specialized cells or structures that detect stimuli and convert them into electrical signals that can be processed by the nervous system.

Different types of receptors are sensitive to specific types of stimuli. For example, photoreceptors (rods and cones) in the eyes detect light, mechanoreceptors (meissner's corpuscles and pacinian corpuscles) in the skin detect touch and pressure, and chemoreceptors (olfactory receptors and taste buds) in the nose and tongue detect chemical stimuli such as odors and tastes respectively.

Summary

The nervous system detects and responds to stimuli through specialized receptors. Irritability is the ability of organisms to sense changes and react, and the CNS and PNS work together to process signals and generate appropriate responses.
