Ecological concepts

Ecology

Ecology is the study of living organisms in relation to their environment.

Environment

This is the surrounding of an organism, which is made up of two parts: physical or non-living or abiotic environment and the living or biotic environment

Habitat

This is the locality in which an organism is adapted to live. It can either be aquatic (water body) or terrestrial (grassland, woodland, soil, farmland etc)

Community

A community is an association of plants and animals of different species that live together in the same habitat, at a particular time, and affect each other.

Population

This is the number of individuals of a particular species living in a habitat at a particular time.

Species

This is a group of similar organisms, which are able to breed together and produce fertile offspring.

Biome

This is a region possessing characteristic physical and climatic conditions, which supports particular species of plants and animals. These organisms show adaptations to these climatic conditions e.g. desert, savannah, tropical evergreen forest

Ecological niche

This is the exact place where an organism lives in a habitat and what it does there e.g. earthworm lives in soil, feeding on dead plant and animal material, and contributing to nutrient reycling

Zonation

This is the distribution of species in space within a habitat, brought about by varying physical conditions, which dictates where each species is able to live.

Ecosystem

An ecosystem is a natural unit, consisting of biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living or physical or climatic) components, that interact to produce a stable and self supporting or perpetuating system e.g. tropical evergreen forest, freshwater, etc.
