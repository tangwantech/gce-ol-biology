Abiotic factors

Abiotic factors are the non-living components of an ecosystem e.g. water, soil, light intensity, rainfall, carbon dioxide concentration, etc

Classification of abiotic factors

Abiotic factors can be classified into three main groups: topographic factors, climatic factors and edaphic factors.

Examples of topographic factors include: gradient of a slope, height of a place, etc

Examples of climatic factors include: temperature, rainfall, light intensity, humidity, wind , etc.

Examples of edaphic factors include: soil water, soil minerals, soil pH, etc

Effects of light on plants

  • Light provides the energy needed by plants for photosynthesis.
  • Some seeds require light to germinate.
  • Light stimulates growth of stem and leaves
  • Light is needed for chlorophyll synthesis
  • Light stimulates bud opening in plants.
  • Light stimulates ripening of fruits
  • Light causes shoots to grow upwards, exposing flowers to agents of pollination, leaves to sunlight for photosynthesis and fruits to agents of dispersal

Effects of light on animals

  • In mammals, light stimulates the skin to synthesize vitamin D from fats. Vitamin D is essential for absorption of calcium in the intestine, which is needed for growth of bones and teeth
  • In humans, light also stimulates the skin to synthesize melanin, which protects us from UV light.
  • Light stimulates photoreceptor cells in the retina of the eye, which enable us to see
  • Most termites swarm during only at night, so avoiding being eaten by birds during the day.
  • Most predators hunt at night to avoid being seen by prey.
  • Some animals move in response to light e.g. cockroaches move away from light
  • Light influences migration, hibernation and reproductive cycles in some animals

Effects of low temperature on plants and animals

Effects of low temperature on plants

  • very low temperature inactivates enzymes
  • It reduces the rate of photosynthesis
  • It reduces the rate of transpiration
  • It stimulates germination in some seeds
  • It reduces the rate of absorption of water and mineral salts from the soil
  • It inhibits ripening of fruits

Effects of low temperature on animals

  • It causes some animals to hibernate
  • It causes mammals to shiver in order to generate heat
  • It slows down metabolic rate
  • It inactivates enzymes

Effects of high temperature on plants

  • It causes leaf fall
  • It increases the rate of transpiration
  • Very high temperature reduces the rate of photosynthesis.
  • It stimulates germination in some seeds.
  • It leads to wilting
  • It stimulates ripening of fruits
  • It stimulates opening of stomata
  • It causes seed dispersal by exposive mechanism
  • It denatures enzymes
  • It increases the rate of absorption of water and mineral salts from the soil

Effects of high temperature on animals

  • It denatures enzymes
  • It increases the rate of sweating and evaporation of water from the skin.
  • It causes some animals to aestivate.

Importance of water to plants and animals

  • Water is a raw material in photosynthesis
  • Water is essential in the diffusion of materials across living membraines
  • Water is used in the hydolysis of organic substances such as proteins, starch, lipids. etc
  • Water serves as a medium for all chemical reactions in cells of plants and animals
  • Water helps in the removal of metabolic waste such as ammonia and urea from the body of animals
  • Water is a component of secretions e.g. saliva, gastric juice, etc
  • Water is the main component of transport medium in animals e.g. blood and lymph
  • Water is the main component of lubricating fluids such as mucus, synovial fluid, pericardial fluid, etc
  • Water provides support in many organisms e.g. hydrostatic skeleton in earthwroms, humours of the eye, amniotic fluid, tugor pressure in herbaceous plants or herbaceous parts of plants, etc.
  • Water helps in temperature regulation such as in evaporation of water during sweating, panting and transpiration that produces a cooling effect.
  • Water helps in seed germination.
  • Water helps in dispersal of some seeds and fruits e.g. coconut
  • Water contributes to cell elongation in plants, which helps in growth
