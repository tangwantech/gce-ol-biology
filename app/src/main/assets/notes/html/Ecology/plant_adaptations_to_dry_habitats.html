Adapations of plants to dry habitat

Plants adapted to grow in dry habitats (e.g. desert) are called xerophytes. They have the following adaptations:

  • Some xerophytes have long slender roots which grow to great depth or extend over wide area. This provides a very large surface area for absroption of water.
  • Many xerophytes have swollen stems or leaves, which contain special water storage tissues.
  • Many shed their leaves to reduce transpiration.
  • They may develop thick waxy cuticle on their leaves to reduce water loss by evaporation.
  • Their stomata may be sunk in pits (sunken stomata), which trap moist air between the stomata and atmosphere, reducing the rate of transpiration.
  • Many xerophytes have small or needle-like leaves. This reduces surface area of the leaves and hence, reducing transpiration.
  • Some xerophytes open their stomata at night and close them during the day to reduce water loss by transpiration.

Summary

Xerophytes are plants adapted to dry habitats with features including deep or widespread roots for water absorption, swollen water-storage tissues, reduced leaf surface area, thick waxy cuticles to minimize water loss, sunken stomata to reduce transpiration, leaf shedding, and nocturnal stomatal opening.
