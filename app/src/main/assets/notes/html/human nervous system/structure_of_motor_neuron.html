Structure of a Motor Neuron

A typical motor neuron consists of three main parts:

Diagram of a motor neuron

  1. Dendrites/Dendrons: These are branched extensions that receive signals from other neurons or sensory receptors.
  2. Cell body (Soma): This contains the nucleus and most of the organelles of the neuron.
  3. Axons: These are long, slender projections that transmit signals away from the cell body to other neurons or target cells. Some axons may be enclosed in a fatty layer called myelin sheath formed by Schwann cells. The myelin sheath is not continuous, having gaps in it known as node of Ranvier. Neurons with myelin sheath are called myelinated neurons, while those without myelin sheath are known as non-myelinated neurons. Neurons with myelin sheath conduct signals faster than those without.

A motor neuron has dendrites, a cell body, and an axon, and may be myelinated to speed transmission. Sensory neurons, motor neurons, and relay neurons differ in cell body placement and the direction in which they carry impulses.
