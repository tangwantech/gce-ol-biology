Synapse

Synapse is the junction between two or more neurons. It allows communication between neurons. The synapse between the axon terminal of a motor neuron and a muscle membrane is called the neuromuscular junction.

Impulse Transmission Across a Synapse

  • When a nerve impulse arrives the synaptic knob, it stimulates synaptic vesicles to release a neurotransmitter e.g. acetylcholine, into the synaptic cleft.
  • The neurotransmitter diffuses across the synaptic cleft and binds to specific receptors on the post-synaptic membrane.
  • The membrane becomes depolarised. When depolarisation reaches the threshold, an action potential is generated and propagated in the post-synaptic neuron.
  • After acetylcholine has carried out its function, an enzyme called cholinesterase destroys it preventing continuous stimulation of the post-synaptic neuron.

Reasons for unidirectional transmission of nerve impulses

1. Neurotransmitters are released only from the pre-synaptic neuron and not from the post-synaptic neuron.

2. Receptors for neurotransmitters are located only on the post-synaptic membrane and not on the pre-synaptic membrane.

3. The refractory period prevents the nerve impulse from traveling backward along the axon.

Summary

At a synapse, the pre-synaptic membrane releases neurotransmitters which diffuse and bind to specific receptors on the post-synaptic membrane, resulting in depolarization and generation of an action potential in the post-synaptic neuron. Cholinesterase destroys neurotransmitters after their function is carried out, preventing continuous stimulation.
