Voluntary and Involuntary Actions

Voluntary Actions

Voluntary actions are those that are under conscious control. These actions require deliberate thought and intention to perform.

Involuntary Actions

Involuntary actions are those that occur without conscious control. These actions are automatic and rapid.

Reflex Action

A reflex action is a rapid, automatic and involuntary response to a specific stimulus without conscious thought.

Importance of Reflex Actions

Reflex actions are essential for protecting the body from harm and maintaining homeostasis.

Examples of a Reflex Action

1. Knee-jerk reflex: When the patellar tendon is tapped, the leg kicks out involuntarily.

2. Pupillary reflex: The pupils constrict in response to bright light and dilate in response to dim light.

3. Withdrawal reflex: withdrawal of the hand when it accidentally touches a hot object.

4. Blink reflex: The eyes blink automatically when a foreign object approaches the eye.

How the body responds when the hand touches a hot or sharp object

When the body encounters a hot or sharp object, a reflex action is triggered to protect the individual from harm. For example, if you touch a hot stove, the withdrawal reflex is activated. The receptor detects the heat, and the sensory neuron transmits the signal to the spinal cord through the dorsal root.

In the gray matter of the spinal cord, the impulse passes across the synapse, aided by acetylcholine (a neurotransmitter) to a relay neuron.

The relay neuron processes the information and sends a signal through another synapse to the motor neuron, aided by acetylcholine.

The motor neuron carries the motor impulses through the ventral root to the bicep muscle of the arm (effector).

The bicep muscle contracts and flexes the arm thereby removing it from the hot or sharp object.

Reflex Arc

A reflex arc is the neural pathway taken by a nerve impulse during a reflex action. It typically involves the following components:

1. Receptor: A specialized structure that detects the stimulus and converts it into an electrical signal.

2. Sensory Neuron: A neuron that carries the electrical signal from the receptor to the central nervous system (CNS).

3. Interneuron: A neuron located within the CNS that processes the information and determines the appropriate response. It connects the sensory neuron to the motor neuron.

4. Motor Neuron: A neuron that carries the electrical signal from the CNS to the effector.

5. Effector: A muscle or gland that produces the response to the stimulus.

Summary

Voluntary actions are controlled by the somatic nervous system and can be learned and modified through practice.

Involuntary actions are controlled by the autonomic nervous system and reflex arcs, and they are inborn or innate.

Reflex actions are rapid, automatic, and involuntary responses to specific stimuli. They are crucial for protecting the body from harm and maintaining homeostasis.

The reflex arc is the neural pathway involved in reflex actions, consisting of a receptor, sensory neuron, interneuron, motor neuron, and effector.
