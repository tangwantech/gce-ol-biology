Biotic interactions

Biotic interactions are the relationships between living organisms in an ecosystem. These interactions shape population size, species distribution, and the flow of energy through food chains and food webs.

Types of biotic interactions

Competition

  • It occurs when two or more organisms need the same limited resource, such as food, light, space, or water. Example: plants competing for sunlight in a dense forest.
  • Competition between organisms of the same species is called Intra-specific competion and competition between individuals of different species is called inter-specific competition.

Predation

  • In predation, one organism (the predator) hunts and eats another organism (the prey). Example: a lion hunting a zebra.
  • Hebivorous animals feeding on grass is known as grazing or browsing.
  • In predation, the predator benefits while the prey is harmed
  • Predators are useful because they can help control the population of organisms that cause harm to us. Predators prevent overpopulation of prey species thereby reducing intra-specific competition. Predators also contribute to natural selection in which weak and less adaptable prey species are eliminated, leaving the most adpated ones.

Parasitism

    Parasitism is the interaction between two living organisms of different species in which, one organism called parasite lives in or on the body of another oganism called host, obtaining food, shelter, and protection, and at the same time causing harm to the host.
  • Parasitism: a parasite lives on or in a host organism and benefits at the host's expense. Example: a tick feeding on a mammal.
  • Mutualism: both organisms benefit from the interaction. Example: bees pollinating flowers while collecting nectar.
  • Commensalism: one organism benefits while the other is neither helped nor harmed. Example: barnacles attached to a whale.
  • Amensalism: one organism is harmed while the other is unaffected. Example: a large tree shading smaller plants.
  • Neutralism: both organisms coexist without affecting each other directly. This is rare but can occur when organisms occupy separate niches.

How biotic interactions affect ecosystems

Biotic interactions influence ecosystem balance by controlling population growth, promoting adaptations, and maintaining biodiversity. They can lead to:

  • Natural selection, as species adapt to compete, avoid predators, or cooperate.
  • Food chain stability, where predator-prey cycles help regulate populations.
  • Habitat structure, because dominant species can alter the environment for others.
  • Species coexistence, through niche differentiation and resource partitioning.

Examples from nature

  • Competition: grass and wildflowers competing for water in a meadow.
  • Predation: eagle feeding on fish in a lake.
  • Parasitism: tapeworms living inside mammals.
  • Mutualism: clownfish and sea anemones protecting each other.
  • Commensalism: orchids growing on tree branches without harming the tree.

Summary

Biotic interactions are the living relationships between organisms that determine how species compete, prey, benefit, or coexist. Important interaction types are competition, predation, parasitism, mutualism, commensalism, and amensalism. Together, these interactions shape ecosystems by influencing population sizes, species behavior, biodiversity, and the flow of energy.
