Adaptations of plants to aquatic habitat

Plants adapated to live in aquatic environment are called hydrophytes. They have the followings features or adaptations, which allow them to survive in such such environment:

  • They have little or no strengthening tissue in the stems and leaf petioles. This provides flexibility in event of changes in water level or water movement.
  • In submerged plants, xylem is greatly reduced, if not absent, because all surface cells appear to absorb water, nutrients and dissolved gases directly from surrounding water. This absorption is further enhanced by the absence of cuticle.
  • Root are often also reduced and their main function is anchorage and not absorption of water and minerals
  • Chloroplasts containing chlorophyll important in photosynthesis are restricted to the upper surface of the leaves, which are the only surface exposed to light
  • The stem contains aerenchyma tissue, which has air filled cavities to provide buoyancy and pathway for diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide to parts of the plant under water.
  • Flowers of most hydrophytes either float on the surface of water or stick out above it such that they can be easily pollinated by wind or insects.

Summary

Hydrophytes are plants adapted to aquatic environments with features including reduced or absent xylem and roots, minimal strengthening tissues for flexibility, reduced cuticles for water and nutrient absorption, chloroplasts restricted to upper leaf surfaces exposed to light, aerenchyma tissue with air-filled cavities for buoyancy and gas diffusion, and flowers positioned to float or emerge above water for pollination.
