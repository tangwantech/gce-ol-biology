Ecological concepts

Ecology

Ecology is the study of living organisms and how they interact with their environment.

Environment

This is the surrounding of an organism, which is made up of the physical (non-living or abiotic) environment and the living (biotic) environment

Habitat

A habitat is the natural environment in which an organism is adapted to live e.g. forest, grassland, desert.

Community

A community is a group (collection) of different species of organisms living together in the same area or habitat and interacting with each other.

Population

A population is a group of individuals of the same species living in the same area (locality) at the same time.

Species

This is a group of similar organisms, which are able to breed together and produce fertile offspring.

Biome

This is a region possessing characteristic physical and climatic conditions, which supports particular species of plants and animals showing adaptations to these climatic conditions e.g. desert, savannah, tropical evergreen forest

Ecological niche

This is the exact place where an organism lives in a habitat and what it does there e.g. earthworm lives in soil, feeding on dead plant and animal material, contributing to nutrient reycling

Zonation

This is the distribution of species in space within a habitat, brought about by varying physical conditions, which dictates where each species is able to live.

Ecosystem

An ecosystem is a natural unit, consisting of all the living organisms (biotic components e.g. plants, animals, microorganisms) and their physical environment (abiotic or non-living components e.g. soil, water, air) interacting with each other to produce a stable and self-sustaining system. e.g. tropical rainforest
