Definition of cell division

Cell division is the process by which a parent cell divides to produce new daughter cells.

Cell division is important in growth, repair and replacement of damaged tissues, asexual reproduction and production of gametes during sexual reproduction.

Chromosomes

  • Chromosomes are thread-like structures, made up of DNA (Deoxyribonucleic acid) and histone proteins. Chromosomes are located in the nucleus of a Eukaryotic cell or in a region of the cytoplasm of a Prokaryotic cell called the nucleoid.
  • Chromosomes carry genes resposible for all the characteristics of an organism.
  • Chromosomes usually occur in pairs(homologous pairs). One member of each pair comes from the male parent while the other comes from the female parent.
  • When a cell is not dividing, chromosomes appear as invisible chromatin threads. When the cell is about to divide, the chromatin threads condense, becoming shorter and thickened. In this state they are known as chromosomes. Each chromosome is seen to consists of two sister chromatids joined together at the centromere.
  • Sister chromatids of a chromosome are genetically identical, and are formed the process of replication.
  • The sister chromatids will eventually be separated between two new daughter cells.
  • The total number of chromosome remains the same in all cells of a given species but varies from one species to another. e.g. human body cell has 46 chromosomes, tomato cells have 24, onion cells have 16, etc.

Diploid (body or somatic) cells

  • Each dipoid cell contains two of every kind of chromosomes (homologous pairs). Homologous chromosomes are similar in shape and structure but are genetically different.
  • The total number of chromosomes in a diploid cell is known as the diploid number and is denoted as 2n. The diploid number is always even.
  • The diploid number in a human body cell is 46 (23 homologous pairs).
  • Diploid cells are important in growth, repair and replacement of damaged tissue, and in asexual reproduction.
  • Examples of diploid cells include Bone cells, muscle cells, nerve cells, liver cells, etc

What are the advantages of possessing two sets of chromosomes?

  • Genetic variation is increased.
  • If a gene on one chromosome of a pair is faulty, the second chromosome may provide a normal backup

Haploid cells (sex cells or gametes)

  • Haploid cells contain only one of each kind of chromosome (chromosomes donot occur in homologous pairs).
  • The total number of chromosomes in a haploid cell is known as the haploid number and is denoted as n. Haploid number is always odd.
  • A human haploid cell contains 23 chromomes
  • Examples of haloid cells include sperm and egg

Similarities between diploid and haploid cells

  • Both contain chromosomes.
