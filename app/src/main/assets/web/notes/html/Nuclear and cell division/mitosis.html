Types of Cell Division

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Describe the process of Mitosis.
  • State the sites of mitosis in plants and animals
  • State the significance of mitosis

1. Mitosis

  • Mitosis is the process by which a parent cell divides to produce two genetically identical daughter cells.
  • It is important for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction in multicellular organisms.

Stages of Mitosis

Interphase

  • DNA replicates and the diploid number of chromosomes doubles (becomes tetraploid)
  • Cell organelles duplicate
  • ATP is accumulated to power the process till the end.
  • The cell grows in size.
  • Centrioles duplicate in animal cells.

    • Interphase

Prophase

  • Chromatin threads condense, becoming shorter, thicker and visible.
  • Each chromosome is seen to consists of two chromatids joined at the centromere.
  • In animal cell, centrioles move to opposite poles of the cell and form the spindle fibers.
  • Nucleolus disappears and nuclear membrane breaks down.

    • Prophase

Metaphase

  • Spindles from opposite poles attach to centromere of each chromosome.
  • Chromosome then align along the equator of the spindles.

    • Metaphase

Anaphase

  • Centromeres split and chromatids separate.
  • Spindle fibers shorten and pull the chromatids (now called chromosomes) apart and towards opposite poles, with centromeres leading.

    • Anaphase

Telophase

  • Chromosomes reach the opposite poles and become invisible chromatin.
  • Nuclear membrane reforms around each set of chromosomes.
  • Nucleolus reappears.
  • Spindle fibers disappear.

    • Telophase

Cytokinesis

  • In animal cells, the cell membrane pinches inwards to form a cleavage furrow along the equator of the cell. The furrow deepens until the cell is pinched into two new genetically identical daughtercells.
  • In plant cells, a cell plate forms across the equator of the cell from the center outwards. The cell plate is formed by fusion of vesicles from the Golgi bodies. Cellulose is the laid down on the cell plate to form a new cell wall, which separates the parent cell into two new genetically identical daughter cells.

    • Cytokinesis

Site of mitosis in plants and animals

  • Mitosis occurs allover the body of an animal. e.g in human mitosis occurs in bones, skin, lining of gut, etc
  • In plants, mitosis occurs in the meristematic regions, such as the tips of roots and shoots.

Significance of mitosis

  • Mitosis is important for growth.
  • Mitosis is important for repair and replacement of damaged tissues.
  • Mitosis is important in asexual reproduction (production of new offspring from by a single parent without the formation of gametes).
  • Mitosis helps to maintain the chromosome number in the offspring.

Summary

Mitosis is the process by which a parent cell divides to produce two genetically identical daughter cells.

It is important for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction in multicellular organisms.

It takes place continuously in the meristematic regions of plants such as the tips of roots and shoots, and in various tissues of animals such as the skin, bone, intestinal lining, etc.
