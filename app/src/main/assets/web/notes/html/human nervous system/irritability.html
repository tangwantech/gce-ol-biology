Irritability

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define irritability
  • Define a stimulus and give examples
  • Define receptors and give examples of different types and the stimuli they detect

Irritability is the ability of living organisms to respond to stimuli.

It is a fundamental characteristic of all living things, enabling them to detect and react to changes (stimuli) in their environment.

Stimulus

A stimulus is any change in the internal or external environment that will bring about a response in an organisms. Examples of stimuli include: light, sound, temperature, chemicals, touch, pressure, etc

Receptors

A receptor is a specialized cell or structure that detects a specific type of stimulus and converts it into an electrical signal that can be processed by the nervous system.

The following table gives examples of different types of receptors, location and the stimuli they detect:

Type of Receptor Location Stimulus Detected
Photoreceptors (rods and cones) Eyes Light
Mechanoreceptors (Meissner's corpuscles and Pacinian corpuscles) Skin Touch and Pressure
Chemoreceptors (Olfactory receptors and Taste buds) Nose and Tongue Chemical stimuli (Odors and Tastes)
Thermoreceptors Skin Temperature
Nociceptors Skin Pain
Organ of Corti Ear Sound
