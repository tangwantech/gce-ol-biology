The Human Eye

Structure of the Human Eye

Vertical section of the human eye

The human eye is a complex organ responsible for vision. It consists of several key structures:

  • The Cornea: The transparent front layer that helps focus light.
  • The Sclera: The white outer layer of the eye. It is tough and provides protection and structural support, helping to maintain the shape of the eye ball.
  • The Choroid: It is the pigmented middle layer of the eye. It contains blood vessels that supply nutrients and oxygen to the retina. The dark pigment it contains prevents internal reflection of light by absorbing it. The anterior end of the choroid is continuous with the ciliary body and iris.
  • The Iris: It contains two sets of muscles (radial and circular ciliary muscles) that adjust the pupil size in response to light conditions.
  • The Pupil: It is the opening in the center of the iris that allows light to enter the eye.
  • The Lens: It is a transparent disc-like structure held in place by suspensory ligaments. It further focuses light onto the retina.
  • The Retina: It is the inner layer of the eye that contains photoreceptor cells (rods and cones) which convert light into electrical impulses.
  • The Optic Nerve: It is a bundle of nerve fibers that transmits visual information from the retina to the brain for processing.
  • Aqueous Humor: It is a clear fluid that fills the space between the cornea and lens and provides nutrients to the cornea and lens. It also helps maintain the shape of the eye.
  • Vitreous Humor: It is a gel-like substance that fills the space between the lens and the retina, helping to maintain the shape of the eye and providing support to the retina.

Difference between rods and cones

Rods Cones
Responsible for vision in dim light (scotopic vision). Responsible for vision in bright light (photopic vision) and color vision.
More sensitive to light. Less sensitive to light.
One type exists. Three types exist (sensitive to red, green, and blue light).
Located mostly in the peripheral regions of the retina. Located mostly in the fovea of the retina.
More numerous. Less numerous.
Photosensitive pigment is rhodopsin. Photosensitive pigments are iodopsins.
Low visual acuity. High visual acuity.
Rapid regeneration of rhodopsin. Slower regeneration of iodopsins.

How the eye is nourished

  • The retina is nourished by the choroid, which contains a network of blood vessels that provide nutrients and oxygen to the retina.
  • The cornea and lens are nourished by diffusion from blood vessels through the aqueous humor, as they have no direct blood supply.

NB: The cornea and lens have no direct blood supply since a network of blood capillaries will impair their ability to focus light.

How the eye is protected

  • Eyelids protect the eye from debris and excessive light.
  • Eyelashes act as a barrier against dust and other particles.
  • Conjunctiva covers and protects the exposed surfaces of the eye.
  • Tears produced by the lacrimal glands help to keep the eye moist and wash away foreign particles.
  • The bony orbit of the skull provides physical protection to the eye.
  • Iris reduce the size of the pupil in bright light, preventing damage to the retina.
  • A thick layer of fat surrounds the eye, providing cushioning and protection.

Summary

The mammalian eye is a complex organ responsible for vision. It consists of several key structures, including the cornea, sclera, choroid, iris, pupil, lens, retina, optic nerve, aqueous humor, and vitreous humor. The eye is nourished by the choroid and diffusion through the aqueous humor. It is protected by eyelids, eyelashes, conjunctiva, tears, the bony orbit of the skull, iris, and a layer of fat surrounding the eye.
