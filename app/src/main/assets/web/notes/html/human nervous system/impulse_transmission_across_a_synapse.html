Impulse Transmission Across a Synapse

Learning Objectives

  • Define a synapse

  • Explain the role of cholinesterase in nerve impulse transmission across synapse
  • Explain why nerve impulse transmission across synpase is unidirectional

Synapse

  • A synapse is a junction between two or more neurons that allows communication between them.

How is nerve impulse transmitted across a synapse?

  • When a nerve impulse arrives the axon terminal, it stimulates synaptic vesicles in the synpatic knob to release a neurotransmitter e.g. acetylcholine, into the synaptic cleft.
  • Acetylcholine diffuses across the synaptic cleft and binds to specific receptors on the post-synaptic membrane of the post synaptic neuron.
  • An action potential is then generated and propagates along the post-synaptic neuron.
  • The enzyme cholinesterase breaks down acetylcholine after action potential has been generated in the post-synaptic neuron. This prevents continuous stimulation of the post-synaptic neuron.

Diagram of a synapse

Reasons why impulse transmission across synapse is unidirectional

  • Neurotransmitters are released only from the pre-synaptic neuron and not from the post-synaptic neuron.
  • Receptors for neurotransmitters are located only on the post-synaptic membrane and not on the pre-synaptic membrane.

Summary

A synapse is the junction between two or more neurons that allows communication between them. Communication occurs through the release of chemicals called neurotransmitters from the presynaptic neuron into the synpatic cleft. Binding of the neurotransmitter to specific receptors on the postsynaptic neuron, depolarizes its membrane and generates an action potential. Acetylcholinesterase breaks down acetylcholine after stimulating the postsynaptic neuron, preventing continuous stimulation of the postsynaptic neuron.
