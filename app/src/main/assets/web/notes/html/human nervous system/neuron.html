Neurons and Nerves

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define a neuron
  • Describe the three main types of neurons and their functions
  • Differentiate between sensory and motor neurons
  • Describe a nerve

A neuron is the basic structural and functional unit of the nervous system. They transmit electrical signals in one direction within the nervous system.

Types of Neurons

There are three main types of neurons namely sensory, relay and motor neurons.

  • Sensory neuron: It transmits signals (nerve impulses) from sensory receptors to the central nervous system.
  • Motor neuron: It transmits nerve impulses from the central nervous system to effectors (muscles or glands).
  • Interneuron (Relay Neuron): It transmits signals from sensory to motor neurons.

Nerve

  • A nerve is a bundle of axons.
  • Individual axons are surrounded by a connective tissue called endoneurium.
  • Groups of axons in a nerve are bound together by the perineurium.
  • An entire nerve is surrounded by another connective tissue called epineurium, which provides protection and support.

Types of nerves

Nerves can be classified into three types based on the type of neurons they contain:

  • Sensory nerves: They are composed of sensory neurons only that carry signals from sensory receptors to the central nervous system.
  • Motor nerves: These are composed of motor neurons only that carry signals from the central nervous system to muscles or glands.
  • Mixed nerves: These contain axons of both sensory and motor neurons, allowing them to transmit signals to and from the central nervous system.

Summary

Neurons are the basic functional units of the nervous system. They carry information from receptors to the central nervous system (CNS), and from the CNS to muscles or glands. Axons of several neurons are held together in bundles by connective tissue to form a nerve.
