Structure of a Motor Neuron

A typical neuron e.g. motor neuron, consists of three main parts namely dendrites, cell body, and axon.

Diagram of a motor neuron

  1. Dendrites/Dendrons: They are branched extensions of the cell body. They receive signals from other neurons or sensory receptors and transmit them to the cell body.
  2. Cell body (Soma): It contains the nucleus and most of the organelles of the neuron. It receives signals from the dendrites and transmits them to the axon.
  3. Axon: It is a long, slender projection from the cell body. It carries nerve impulses away from the cell body to other neurons or target cells. Some axons may be enclosed in a fatty layer of myelin sheath formed by Schwann cells. The Myelin sheath acts as an insulator, increasing the speed of impulse transmission. Myelin sheath is not continuous, having gaps in it known as nodes of Ranvier. These allow for saltatory conduction (jumping of action potentials from node to node), enabling rapid transmission of nerve impulses. Neurons with myelin sheath are called myelinated neurons, while those without myelin sheath are known as non-myelinated neurons.

Summary

A neuron e.g. motor neuron consists of dendrites, a cell body, and an axon. Dendrites receive signals from other neurons or sensory receptors and send them to the cell body, while the axon carries the signals away from the cell body to other neurons.
