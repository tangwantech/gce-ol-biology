Structure of a Motor Neuron

A typical motor neuron consists of three main parts:

Diagram of a motor neuron

  1. Dendrites/Dendrons: These are branched extensions that receive signals from other neurons or sensory receptors.
  2. Cell body (Soma): This contains the nucleus and most of the organelles of the neuron. It processes the incoming signals from the dendrites.
  3. Axons: These are long, slender projections that transmit signals away from the cell body to other neurons or target cells. Some axons may be enclosed in a fatty layer called myelin sheath formed by Schwann cells. Myelin sheath acts as an insulating layer, increasing the speed of impulse transmission. The myelin sheath is not continuous, having gaps in it known as node of Ranvier. This allows for saltatory conduction (jumping of action potentials from node to node). Neurons with myelin sheath are called myelinated neurons, while those without myelin sheath are known as non-myelinated neurons. Myelinated neurons conduct impulses faster than non-myelinated neurons.

Summary

A motor neuron has dendrites, a cell body, and an axon, and may be myelinated to speed transmission. Dendrites receive signals and send them to the cell body, the cell body processes the signals, and the axon transmits the signals away from the cell body to other neurons.
