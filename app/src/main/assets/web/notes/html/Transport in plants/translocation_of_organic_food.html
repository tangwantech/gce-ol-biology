Translocation of Organic Food

  • The mass flow hypothesis can be used to explain how translocation of organic food takes place in the phloem.
  • According to the mass flow hypothesis, solutes flow through the phloem from a region of high hydrostatic pressure to a region of low hydrostatic pressure.
  • The leaves which, carry out photosynthesis act as the source of sugar (sucrose).
  • The sugar is actively pumped into the phloem sieve tubes by companion cells. This lowers the water potential in the sieve tubes.
  • Water therefore enters the seive tubes by osmosis, causing a high hydrostatic pressure to develop in the seive tubes near the leaves.
  • The high hydrostatic pressure causes sugar solution to flow down the phloem to the roots.
  • The sucrose passes from the phloem of the roots into the root cells. Water follows by osmosis.
  • As there is less water in the sieve tubes of the roots, a lower hydrostatic pressure is developed here.
  • In the root cells, the sugar is either respired or converted to starch and stored. The roots can be described as sinks because they remove sugar from the phloem
  • As sugar in the sink decreases, its water potential increases and water therefore leaves the sink into surrounding cells with lower water potential by osmosis.
  • Both the sink and seive tubes in the phloem develop a lower hydrostatic pressure and thus, a pressure gradient is established between the sieve tubes close to the leaves and sieve tubes close to the roots.
  • As the source and sink are iinked by phloem, sucrose solution flows from high hydrostatic pressure in the leaves to low hydrostatic pressure in the roots.
  • Tranlocation in phloem can be bi-directional (from the leaves to the roots or from roots to actively growing regions).

Some properties of sucrose that makes it suitable for transport in the phloem

  • It is very soluble in water hence, large quantity can be transported in the phloem.
  • It is inert or unreactive and is therefore not used up during its transport from the leaves to the roots

Summary

Translocation of organic food substances in the phloem is by mass flow hypothesis. This invloves the movement of solutes from a region of high hydrostatic pressure.

The leaves are described as the source because the produce sugars while the roots are the sinks because the remove sugar from the phloem.

Sucrose is the main form in which carbohydrate is transported in the phloem in plants because it is soluble in water and metabolically inert or unreactive.
