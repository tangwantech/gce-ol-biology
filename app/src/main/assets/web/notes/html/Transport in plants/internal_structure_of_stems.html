Internal Structure of Stems

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Describe or draw labeled diagrams of the internal structure of monocot and dicot stems.
  • Differentiate between the internal structure of monocot and dicot stems

Stems

Stems are the main organ involved in the transport of water, mineral salts and organic compounds between the roots and leaves. Stems also help to support the leaves, flowers and fruits.

Although stems of both monocots and dicots have the same tissues, the pattern of arrangement of these tissues in each is markly different.

Internal structure of monocot stem

  • The internal structure of a monocot stem is characterized by scattered vascular bundles, which are surrounded by ground tissue.
  • The vascular bundles contain the vascular tissues, including the xylem and phloem.
  • The xylem is located towards the inner side of the vascular bundle, while the phloem is located towards the outer side of the vascular bundle.
  • The ground tissue is composed of parenchyma cells and occupies the space between the vascular bundles.
  • Surrounding the ground tissues and vascular bundles is the epidermis, which protects the internal tissues from mechanical injury and infection.

Internal structure of a monocot stem

Internal structure of dicot stem

  • The internal structure of a dicot stem is characterized by vascular bundles arranged in a ring, which surrounds a central ground tissue called pith.
  • The pith is composed of parenchyma cells and occupies the center of the stem.
  • The vascular bundles contain the vascular tissues, including the xylem and phloem.
  • The xylem is located towards the inner side of the vascular bundle, while the phloem is located towards the outer side of the vascular bundle.
  • Cambium is found between the xylem and phloem and contributes to secondary growth of the stem.
  • The ground tissue is composed of parenchyma cells and occupies the space between the vascular bundles.
  • Surrounding the ground tissues and vascular bundles is the epidermis, which protects the internal tissues from mechanical injury and infection.

Internal structure of a dicot stem

Differences between the internal structure of monocot and dicot stems

Monocot stem Dicot stem
Vascular bundles are scattered Vascular bundles are arranged in a ring
No distinct cortex and pith Distinct cortex and pith present
Xylem and phloem are not separated by cambium Xylem and phloem are separated by cambium
No secondary growth Secondary growth occurs
