Mechanism of Uptake and Ascend of Water

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Explain the mechanism of water uptake by roots.
  • Describe the mechanism of water ascent in plants.

Mechanism of Water Uptake by Roots

  • Soil has a higher water potential than the root hair cells. Water therefore moves from the soil into the root hair cells by osmosis.
  • The thin root hair cells provide a large surface area for absorption of water.
  • While in the root, water moves through the cells of the cortex by three different pathways: apoplast pathway (through the cell walls), vacuolar pathway (through the vacuoles), and symplast pathway (through the cytoplasm).
  • From the cortex, water moves through the endodermis into the root xylem by the symplast pathway.

Water uptake by roots

Mechanism of Water Ascent in Plants

  • Continuous absorption of water by the root hair cells generates root pressure in the xylem, which helps to push water upward to a certain height in the xylem of the stem.
  • Continuous loss of water from the leaves (transpiration) creates a negative pressure (suction pressure) in the xylem, which pulls water up the xylem of the stem to the xylem of the leaves.
  • The cohesive forces between water molecules, allows water to flow up the xylem in a continuous column.
  • The adhesive forces between water molecules and the wall of the xylem vessels, causes water to adhere to the walls, which helps to counteract the force of gravity, allowing water to flow up the xylem by capillary action.
  • Suction pressure is the main driving force for the ascent of water in plants.
  • Lignin in the wall of the xylem vessels prevents the collapse of the vessels as water flows through them under tension.
  • From the xylem of the leaves, water flows form one leaf cell to another by osmosis. Some of the water is used for metabolic processes e.g. photosynthesis, while excess is eventually lost to the atmosphere through the stomata, by transpiration.

Absorption of mineral salts

  • The concentration of mineral salts in the soil is lower than that in the root hair cells.
  • The root hair cells therefore absorb mineral salts from the soil against a concentration gradient by active transport. This requires energy in the form of ATP.
  • Once in the root, mineral salts are transported to the xylem dissolved in water.
  • Mineral salts enter the xylem by active transport and are carried to the leaves in transpiration stream

Summary

Root hair cells absorb water from the soil by osmosis. Mineral salts are absorbed by active transport. Water and mineral salts then move through the cortex of the root to the xylem by three different pathways: apoplast, vacuolar and symplast pathways. Water is then transported up the xylem of the stem to the leaves by root pressure and suction pressure. The cohesive and adhesive forces of water molecules help to maintain a continuous column of water in the xylem vessels, allowing water to flow up the xylem by capillary action. Once in the leaf, water moves through the leaf cells by osmosis and excess is eventually lost to the atmosphere through the stomata, by transpiration.
