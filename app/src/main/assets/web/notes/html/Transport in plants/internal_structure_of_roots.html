Roots

Roots are the main organ involved in the absorption of water and mineral salts from the soil. Roots also help to anchor the plant firmly in the soil.

Although roots of both monocots and dicots have the same tissues, the pattern of arrangement of these tissues in each is markly different.

Internal structure of monocot root

  • The internal structure of a monocot root is characterized by a central stele surrounded by an endodermis,a cortex and a piliferous layer.
  • The stele contains the vascular tissues, including the xylem and phloem.
  • The xylem is arranged in a ring around the central pith, while the phloem is located between the xylem arms.
  • The pith is composed of parenchyma cells and occupies the central part of the root.
  • The endodermis is a single layer of cells that surrounds the stele and regulates the movement of substances into the vascular tissue.
  • The cortex is the layer of cells between the piliferous layer and the endodermis and is involved in the storage of nutrients and transport of water from root hairs towards the root xylem.
  • The piliferous layer is the outermost layer of the root, and gives rise to the root hairs.

Internal structure of dicot root

  • The internal structure of a dicot root is characterized by a central stele surrounded by an endodermis, a cortex and a piliferous layer.
  • The stele contains the vascular tissues, including the xylem and phloem.
  • The xylem is arranged in a star shape with the phloem located between the arms of the xylem.
  • The endodermis is a single layer of cells that surrounds the stele and regulates the movement of substances into the vascular tissue.
  • The cortex is the layer of cells between the piliferous layer and the endodermis and is involved in the storage of nutrients and transport of water from root hairs towards the root xylem.
  • The piliferous layer is the outermost layer of the root, and gives rise to the root hairs.
