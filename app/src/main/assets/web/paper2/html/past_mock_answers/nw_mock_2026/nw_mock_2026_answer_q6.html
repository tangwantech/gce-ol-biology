NorthWest Mock 2026 - Answer Q6

  1. What are the characteristics of insect pollinated flowers?

    (4 marks)
    • Flowers are usually large and conspicuous.
    • Brightly colored petals to attract insects.
    • Presence of nectar to attract pollinators.
    • Some produce sweet smells to attract insects.
    • Pollen grains are sticky to adhere to the bodies of insects.
    • Athers are firmly attached to filaments and held in position, which allows them to come into contact with visiting insects.
  2. Cell division is fundamental to the life cycle of a flowering plant.

    1. Briefly explain the significance of mitosis in plant growth.

      (6 marks)
      • Growth (increase in size of plant due to addition of new cells) is achieved by mitosis
      • Germination of seeds into a young plant is by mitosis.
      • Development of zygote into embryo is by mitosis.
      • Increase in length of roots and shoots is by mitosis.

    2. Describe the process and importance of meiosis in the formation of gametes in flowering plants.

      (5 marks)

      Formation of male gametes (pollen grains)

      • Formation of pollen grains takes place in the anthers
      • Each diploid pollen mother cell undergoes two rounds of meiotic divisions to form four haploid microspores
      • Each microspore then develops into a pollen gain

      Formation of female gametes

      • Inside the ovule is a diploid megaspore mother cell
      • The megaspore mother cell undergoes meiosis to produce four haploid megaspores
      • Three of the megaspores degenerate, leaving one functional megaspore.
      • The functional megaspore undergoes mitotic divisions to form the embryo sac, which consists of 8 haploid nuclei (2 synergids, 1 egg nucleus, 2 polar nuclei, and 3 antipodal nuclei)
      • The egg nucleus becomes the female gamete.

      Importance of meiosis in gamete formation

      • Meiosis reduces diploid number of chromososmes to haploid number, preventing doubling during fertilization in every generation.
      • Crossing over and independent assortment during meiosis results in genetic variation in resulting gametes and offspring

  3. State how fertilization occurs in a flowering plant.

    (5 marks)
    • When the pollen tube reaches the micropyle, its tip breaks open, the tube nucleus degenerates and the two male gametes are released into embryo sac in the ovule
    • One of the male gamete fuses with the egg nucleus to form a diploid zygote
    • The other male gamete fuses with the two polar nuclei at the center of the embryo sac to form a triploid primary endosperm nuclues
    • Because fertilization occurs twice, it is also known as double fertilization.

  4. How is water important in seed germination?

    (5 marks)
    • Water activates protoplasm and enzymes<./li>
    • Water is essential for elongation of new cells.
    • Water is used to hydrolyse stored food e.g. starch to glucose which is used in tissue respiration to provide energy.
    • Water dissolves and transports soluble food substances to growing regions of the plumule and radicle.
    • Water causes the seed to swell, which facilated breaking of the seed coat for the plumule and radicle to emerge
    • Water softens seed coat.
    • Water serves as medium for chemical reactions.
