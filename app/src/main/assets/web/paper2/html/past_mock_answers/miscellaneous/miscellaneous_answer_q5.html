  2. How does blood function in transport and defense?

    Function of blood in transport:

    • Transports oxygen form lungs to tissues for respiration
    • Transports carbon dioxide from tissues to lungs for excretion
    • Transports hormones from endocrine glands to target organs
    • Transports end products of digestion from the intestines to the liver and other parts of the body
    • Transports nitrogenous wastes e.g urea to the kidneys for excretion
    • Transports heat throughout the body to help regulate body temperature
    • Transports drugs from points of administration to infected areas

    Function of blood in defense:

    • Blood contains white blood cells e.g. phagocytes that engulf and destroy pathogens.
    • White blood cells called lymphocytes produce antibodies that help neutralize and destroy specific pathogens
    • Blood clot formation prevents entry of pathogens and excessive bleeding when blood vessels are damaged

  3. Describe the mechanism of blood clotting

    Mechanism of blood clotting:

    1. When a blood vessel is injured, platelets adhere to the exposed collagen fibers at the site of injury, forming a temporary platelet plug.
    2. Damaged tissues and platelets release clotting factors e.g. thromboplastin, on exposure to air.
    3. Thromboplastin stimulates the conversion of prothrombin (a plasma protein) into thrombin (an enzyme). This requires calcium ions.
    4. Thrombin then converts fibrinogen (a soluble plasma protein) into fibrin (an insoluble protein), which forms a mesh-like structure that traps blood cells and strengthens the platelet plug, resulting in the formation of a stable blood clot.
    5. The blood clot later dries to form a scab, beneath which tissue repair continues to completely seal the wound or damage blood vessel.
    6. The scab eventually falls off as the underlying tissue heals.
