  1. Draw a large and well labelled diagram of the male reproductive system of man.

    Diagram of the male reproductive system of man

  2. State the functions of the parts labelled.

    • Scrotum: Protects, supports and regulates the temperature of the testes
    • Testes: Produce sperm and testosterone
    • Epididymis: Stores and matures sperm
    • Vas deferens: Transports sperm from the epididymis to the urethra
    • Prostate gland: Produces an alkaline fluid that neutralizes the acidity of the urethra and vagina
    • Seminal vesicles: Produce seminal fluid rich in fructose. Sperm break down fructose during respiration to produce energy.
    • Urethra: Transports urine and sperm out of the body
    • Penis: used for copulation and urination

  3. What are the secondary sexual characteristics of a human female?

    • Development of breasts
    • Widening of hips
    • Growth of hair on the pubic and armpit regions
    • Onset of menstruation
    • Change in body shape
    • Ovulation begins
    • Appearance of fat deposits under the skin, especially around the hips and thighs.

  4. What are the functions of the placenta?

    • Transfers nutrients from the mother to the fetus
    • Transfers oxygen from the mother to the fetus
    • Transfers waste products from the fetus to the mother for excretion
    • Supplies antibodies from the mother to the fetus
    • Produces hormones e.g. progesterone and oestrogen that maintain the pregnancy

  5. What are the functions of amniotic fluid?

    • Acts as a shock absorber, preventing mechanical damage (injury) to the fetus
    • Maintains a constant temperature and pressure around the fetus
    • Lubricates the birth canal during delivery
    • Allows for foetal movement and development
    • Allows for proper lung development

  6. Describe the birth process in humans.

    • Towards the end of gestation, the baby rotates such that its head is positioned downward towards the cervix.
    • Under the influence of oxytocin from the posterior pituitary, the uterus begins to contract involuntarily. This is known as labour.
    • The contractions are at first irregular and less intense but become stronger and more frequent as labour progresses.
    • The amniotic sac ruptures, releasing the amniotic fluid, which is known as water breaking. Amniotic fluid helps to lubricate the birth canal.
    • Relaxin secreted by the placenta causes the cervix to dilate and the ligaments of the pelvis become more flexible.
    • Contraction of the uterus and abdominal muscles pushes the baby through the birth canal with the head of the baby leading.
    • Once the baby is delivered, the umbilical cord is clamped and cut close to the baby's abdomen.
    • After delivery of the baby, the placenta is expelled from the uterus together with the remaining umbilical cord. This is known as the afterbirth.

  7. Differentiate between sexual and asexual reproduction.

    Sexual Reproduction Asexual Reproduction
    Involves two parents Involves a single parent
    Involves the fusion of male and female gametes (fertilization) No fusion of gametes (No fertilization)
    Offspring show genetic variation Offspring are genetically identical to the parent
    Involves meiosis to produce gametes Involves mitosis for reproduction
    Involves formation of zygote No zygote formation
    Slow method of reproduction Fast method of reproduction
    Fewer offspring are produced Many offspring are produced
