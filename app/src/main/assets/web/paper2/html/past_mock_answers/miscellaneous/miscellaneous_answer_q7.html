  1. Define the following terms as used in ecology

    1. Ecosystem

      An ecosystem is a natural unit, consisting of all the living organisms (biotic components e.g. plants, animals, microorganisms) and their physical environment (abiotic or non-living components e.g. soil, water, air) interacting with each other to produce a stable and self-sustaining system. e.g. tropical rainforest

    2. Community

      A community is a group (collection) of different species of organisms living together in the same area or habitat and interacting with each other.

    3. Habitat

      A habitat is the natural environment in which an organism is adapted to live e.g. forest, grassland, desert.

    4. Population

      A population is a group of individuals of the same species living in the same area (locality) at the same time.

  2. Describe how carbon circulates in nature

    • Carbon dioxide in the atmosphere is absorbed by plants during photosynthesis to produce carbohydrates and other organic compounds.
    • Animals consume plants (or other animals) and use the carbon in their bodies for energy and growth.
    • Also, organisms such as corals in seas absorb carbon dioxide dissolved in the water and turn it into calcium carbonate, used to build their shells and skeletons. When these organisms die, the calcium carbonate is deposited on the ocean floor, forming sedimentary rocks, and locking in carbon.
    • When plants and animals respire, they release carbon dioxide back into the atmosphere.
    • When plants and animals die, decomposers break down their bodies, releasing carbon back into the soil and atmosphere.
    • Human activities such as burning fossil fuels, deforestation and bush burning, release large amounts of carbon dioxide back into the atmosphere.
    • Natural activities such as volcanic eruptions can also release carbon dioxide into the atmosphere.

  3. Describe how nitrogen circulates in nature

    • Nitrogen gas (N₂) in the atmosphere is converted into ammonia (NH₃) by nitrogen-fixing bacteria e.g. Rhizobium in the soil.
    • Thunderstorms convert nitrogen gas into Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), which combine with raindrops to form nitric acid(HNO₃ or acid rain), which falls to the ground. While in the soil, the nitric acid combines with minerals to form nitrates (NO₃⁻).
    • Man made fertilizers also add nitrates to the soil.
    • Plants absorb nitrogen from the soil in the form of nitrates (NO₃⁻) and use it to build proteins and nucleic acids.
    • Animals consume plants and use the nitrogen in their bodies for growth and development.
    • When plants and animals die, decomposers break down their bodies, releasing nitrogen back into the soil as ammonium (NH₄⁺).
    • Animals also release nitrogen waste, which can be converted into ammonium by decomposers.
    • Nitrifying bacteria e.g. Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter convert ammonium into nitrates, which can be absorbed by plants again.
    • Denitrifying bacteria convert nitrates back into nitrogen gas, which is released into the atmosphere.
    • Volcanic eruptions can also release nitrogen compounds into the atmosphere.

  4. Describe the flow of energy in an ecosystem

    • Energy flows through an ecosystem in one direction, from the sun to producers (plants) to consumers (animals) to decomposers.
    • Producers capture solar energy and convert it into chemical energy through photosynthesis.
    • Consumers obtain the energy by eating plants or other organisms.
    • Some of the energy is lost as heat at each level of the food chain.
    • Decomposers break down dead organisms and release energy back into the environment as heat.
    • Only about 10% of the energy is transferred from one level to the next, while the rest is lost as heat.
