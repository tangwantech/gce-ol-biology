West Mock 2026 - Question 2

  1. Define the following

    1. Enzyme (4 marks)

      An enzyme is an organic or biological catalyst, protein in nature and secreted by living cells to speed up the rate of biochemical reactions but are not used up in course of the reaction. They also remain chemically unchanged at the end of the reaction. e.g. amylase, protease, lipase etc.

    2. Respiration (5 marks)

      Respiration is a series of biochemical reactions in which complex organic substances such as carbohydrates, fats and proteins are broken down by enzymes with or without the use of oxygen to release water, carbon dioxide, energy(ATP) and sometimes alcohol in yeast and plants or lactic acid in animals.

  2. Enzymes work most efficiently within a narrow temperature range and at specific pH. Describe an experiment to demonstrate one of the characteristics of enzymes above. (6 marks)

    Both experiments are described below however, you are expected to describe only one.

    Experiment to demonstrate the effect of temperature on enzyme activity:

    Aim: To investigate the effect of temperature on the activity of an enzyme.

    Requirements: Two tests tubes labelled A and B, enzyme solution (saliva), substrate solution (starch), water bath, iodine solution, dropper, stopwatch, sodium carbonate solution.

    Procedure:

    1. Add 2cm³ of saliva to each test tube.
    2. Place test tube A in boiling water for 5 minutes and then cool the test tube under running water. Test tube A is the control experiment while B is the test experiment.
    3. Add 2cm³ of starch solution to each test tube.
    4. Add 3 drops of sodium carbonate solution to both test tubes to maintain alkaline conditions.
    5. Place both test tubes in a water bath at 36°C for 30 minutes. Time using a stopwatch.
    6. After 30 minutes, get a sample from each test tube and test for the presence of starch with iodine solution.

    Observation: The control experiment (A) will show a positive test for starch (turns blue-black), while the test experiment (B) will show a negative result for starch (no colour change or remains brown).

    Explanation: The control experiment (A) showed a positive test for starch because the enzyme (saliva) was denatured by boiling, hence starch was not converted to maltose. On the other hand, the test experiment (B) showed a negative test for starch because the enzyme was active and converted starch to maltose.

    Conclusion: Temperature affects the activity of enzymes.

    Experiment to demonstrate the effect of pH on enzyme activity:

    Aim: To investigate the effect of pH on enzyme activity.

    Requirements: Two tests tubes labelled A and B, enzyme solution (saliva), substrate solution (starch), water bath, iodine solution, dropper, stopwatch, sodium carbonate solution, hydrochloric acid (HCL) solution.

    Procedure:

    1. Add 2cm³ of saliva to each test tube.
    2. Add 3 drops of HCL to test tube A. Test tube A is the control experiment
    3. Add 2cm³ of starch solution to each test tube.
    4. Add 3 drops of sodium carbonate solution to test tube B to maintain alkaline conditions. Test tube B is the test experiment.
    5. Place both test tubes in a water bath at 36°C for 30 minutes. Time using a stopwatch.
    6. After 30 minutes, get a sample from each test tube and test for the presence of starch with iodine solution.

    Observation: The control experiment (A) will show a positive test for starch (turns blue-black), while the test experiment (B) will show a negative result for starch (no colour change or remains brown).

    Explanation: The control experiment (A) showed a positive test for starch because the enzyme (saliva) was denatured by HCL, hence starch was not converted to maltose. On the other hand, the test experiment (B) showed a negative test for starch because the enzyme was active and converted starch to maltose.

    Conclusion: pH affects the activity of enzymes.
