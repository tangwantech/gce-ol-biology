West Mock 2026 - Answer Q7

  1. Write short notes on the following

    1. Stimulus (3 marks)

      A stimulus is any change in the internal or external environment of an organism that brings about a response in the organism. Examples include light, sound, temperature, touch, chemicals etc.

    2. Reflex action (3 marks)

      A reflex action is a rapid, involuntary and automatic response to a stimulus that does not involve the conscious part of the brain. It is a protective mechanism that helps an organism to respond quickly to danger or harmful stimuli. Examples include blinking when an object approaches the eye, pulling back the hand when it accicdentally touches a hot object etc.

    3. Coordination (3 marks)

      Coordination is the process by which different parts of the body work together as one to maintain homeostasis and respond to stimuli effectively. Coordination is achieved through the nervous system and endocrine system.

  2. Describe what happens when the hand accidentally touches a hot object. (6 marks)

    1. When the hand acciddentally touches a hot or sharp object, pain receptors in the hand are stimulated, and the stimulus is converted into a nerve impulse.
    2. The impulse is then transmitted along a sensory neuron to the relay neuron in the spinal cord.
    3. The arrival of the nerve impulse at the axon terminal of the sensory neuron stimulates the release of neurotransmitter e.g. acetylcholine into the synapse.
    4. Acetylcholine diffuses across the synapse and stimulates the relay neuron, generating a nerve impulse in the relay neuron.
    5. The nerve impulse is propagated along the relay neuron and when it arrives the axon terminal, acetylcholine is again release into another synapse
    6. The acetylcholine diffuses across the synapse and triggers a nerve impulse in a motor neuron, which leaves the spinal cord through the ventral root to the bicep of the arm.
    7. The bicep is then stimulated to contract while the tricep relaxes, flexing the arm and pulling the hand away from the hot or sharp object

  3. Distinguish between nervous and hormonal response. (5 marks)

    Nervous response Hormonal response
    Fast-acting Slower-acting
    Short-lived Long-lasting
    Information is electrochemical (nerve impulses) Information is chemical (hormones)
    Information is transmitted through neurons Information is transmitted through the bloodstream
    Response is localized to specific target organs or tissues Response can affect multiple target organs or tissues throughout the body
    Examples include reflex actions, voluntary movements, etc. Examples include regulation of blood sugar levels by insulin, osmoregulation, etc.
