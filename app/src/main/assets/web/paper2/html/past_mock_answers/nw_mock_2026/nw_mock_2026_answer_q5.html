NorthWest Mock 2026 - Answer Q5

  1. Define the following

    1. Reflex action (3 marks)

      A reflex action is a rapid, automatic and involuntary response to a stimulus without conscious thought. e.e. withdrawal of the hand when it accidentally touches a hot object.

    2. Reflex arc (3 marks)

      A reflex arc is the neural pathway taken by a nerve impulse during a reflex action. It involves a sensory receptor, sensory neuron, interneuron (in the spinal cord), motor neuron and an effector (muscle or gland).

    3. Tropism (3 marks)

      Tropism is the growth movement of part of a stationary plant in response to a unidirectional source of stimulus. It can be positive (towards the stimulus) or negative (away from the stimulus). Examples include phototropism (response to light) and geotropism (response to gravity).

  2. Describe how movement is brought about at the elbow joint in the fore arm. (6 marks)

    • Elbow joint is a hinge joint that allows flexion and extension movements only in one plane.
    • Movement at the elbow joint is brought about by the action of biceps and triceps (antagonistic muscles).
    • In order to bend the fore arm, the biceps muscle contracts while the triceps muscle relaxes. The bicep pulls the radius towards the humerus, resulting in flexion of the fore arm.
    • In order to straighten the fore arm, the triceps muscle contracts while the biceps muscle relaxes. The tricep pulls the ulna away from the humerus, resulting in extension of the fore arm.

  3. How is the human skeleton adapted to perform its function? (5 marks)

    • The human skeleton is composed of hard bones that provide structural support and protection for internal organs.
    • Bones contain deposits of calcium and phosphorus, which give them their hardness and strength.
    • Joints between bones, allow for movement and flexibility in various directions.
    • Bone marrow within long bones serve as sites for blood cell production
    • Trabeculae at the end of long bones serve as shock absorbers.
    • Some bones have points for attachment of skeletal muscles and ligaments.
